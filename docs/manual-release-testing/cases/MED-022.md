1. [ ] **MED-022 — Explicit attachment download in background** — On Android 14 or later with a disposable account and an uncached large attachment, tap Download, Save, or Open while the app is visible, then immediately switch apps and lock the screen. Repeat with notifications denied, a metered network, rotation, process recreation, Cancel, and a second deliberate tap. During a 30–50 MiB APK transfer, disconnect and reconnect the network twice so Android stops and restarts the same transfer job; cancel the restarted run, then deliberately retry it. On a disposable Android 14+ install, also resume a previously queued interactive WorkManager download or use a test build that makes user-initiated job scheduling fail, so the foreground-worker fallback actually runs. On Android 11–13, repeat with the foreground-worker fallback. Observe the generic download notification and compare final bytes with the source. Also tap Download on two different uncached attachments at once, tap one of them several more times, and download a tiny or already cached file; watch the status bar and shade with sound and vibration on, and note the summary line while both transfers run and after one of them finishes. Force-stop the app while two transfers are live, then relaunch it and look at the shade. Afterwards read the `DMAttachmentTransfers` lines in logcat. After upgrading over an earlier build, open the app's notification settings once. Repeat several completed and failed downloads and inspect the shade afterward. Also let an automatic (non-tapped) download be stopped by Android repeatedly, for example by toggling the network several times, then tap Download on that same attachment while it is waiting. Companion automated coverage cancels the shared fetch or times it out while its Android owner remains active, and separately stops or replaces that owner during cancellation cleanup. → **Expected:** An explicit transfer continues after ordinary backgrounding and completes once in the encrypted cache and requested destination; the notification exposes no account, chat, filename, or media content and disappears when its transfer completes or fails, including a cancelled shared fetch whose Android owner is still active. The card is quiet: it shows an indeterminate progress bar, never makes a sound, vibration or heads-up banner when it updates, and a transfer that finishes within a few seconds may show no card or only a brief one, which is recorded as an observation and is not a failure, because Android does not guarantee that a deferred card stays hidden. After an upgrade the earlier Downloading channel is replaced by a single new one with no badge, unless it was blocked, in which case it stays blocked. One attachment keeps one card however many times it is tapped or retried, and two different attachments show two cards that disappear as each finishes, collapsed under one quiet summary that reads "2 attachments downloading" (three transfers read "3 attachments downloading"), never makes a sound or vibration, and disappears when only one transfer remains, so a single transfer never shows a summary. A summary left behind by a force-stopped app is gone after relaunch and in any case expires within half an hour. Each run leaves one begin and one end line in the `DMAttachmentTransfers` log with a process-local sequence number, the owner (user-initiated job or work manager), the attempt and the outcome (completed, failed, retrying or stopped), and a retry after a stop or a manual tap appears as a new sequence rather than a rewritten one; the lines never contain an account, conversation, message, file name or attachment index. Completed attachments never leave download-completion cards or an accumulating stack. A cancelled fetch clears that transfer's interactive intent and permits a deliberate retry; Android stopping the owner preserves its retry intent. A stopped job's delayed cleanup cannot unregister or finish its replacement, and the restarted run remains cancellable. The fallback declares the `dataSync` foreground-service type and never crashes or loops with `InvalidForegroundServiceTypeException`. Cancel stops that transfer and suppresses automatic restart; a later deliberate tap can retry. Automatic transfers still honor pause and network policy. A real scheduler stop must preserve native retry ownership. Verify compatible committed-prefix Range/If-Range recovery separately from background continuation; an incompatible representation must discard the partial body safely. WorkManager stop diagnostics include a coarse reason and zero-based run attempt, never attachment identity or exception context; that scheduler count is not a native retry budget. An automatic transfer that Android stops resumes after a growing delay (30 seconds, doubling per run, at most five hours) instead of restarting at once, so a stop burst does not become hundreds of restarts; a transfer persisted by the previous release adopts the same delay the next time it runs. A single transient failure after any number of stops still gets its one follow-up attempt. A deliberate tap, open or save on that attachment replaces the waiting automatic job and starts without waiting out its delay, and repeating the tap or a later automatic request coalesces onto the same transfer. Tapping twice quickly neither restarts nor duplicates the transfer, and an automatic transfer that was running when a deliberate tap handed it to the foreground job, or when it was cancelled, never reappears later as a second download. No test uninstalls the app or clears its data.
